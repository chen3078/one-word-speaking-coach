# Watermelon Behavioral Example

This is a behavioral example, not a script to reveal before the learner practices.

## Silent lesson plan

- Topic type: food and plant
- Target patterns: `It is ...`, `It has ...`, `We can ...`, `It grows ...`, `I like ... because ...`
- Useful words: watermelon, fruit, green, red, rind, flesh, seed, slice, spoon, juice, summer, supermarket
- Main questions: identity, size, color, parts, edible parts, season, purchase place, eating method, growth, preference
- Retelling route: fruit → size/color → rind/flesh/seeds → summer → buy → eat/drink → grow → preference
- Transfer topic: apple

The lesson should not stop at facts the learner volunteers. If the learner names only one purchase place, teach common alternatives such as a supermarket, roadside fruit stand, and fruit shop. If the learner does not know the growth process, teach a simple sequence such as `seed → sprout → vine → flower → fruit`, add brief Chinese notes for new terms, and require a retry.

## Expected interaction shape

Coach: `Let’s talk about watermelon. What kind of food is it?`

Learner: `Fruit.`

Coach: `Yes. Say: “A watermelon is a fruit.” Your turn.`

Learner: `A watermelon is a fruit.`

Coach: `Good. What color is it?`

Learner: `It is green outside and red inside.`

Coach: `Exactly. What does it have inside?`

Continue one question at a time. Do not display the remaining questions.

## Retelling phase

Show only:

`fruit → big → green/red → rind/flesh/seeds → summer → supermarket → slices/spoon/juice → seed-to-fruit → preference`

Prompt:

`Now use these keywords to talk about watermelon for 30 to 60 seconds.`

After the lesson, create an English-only review card when visual generation is available. Use a central watermelon illustration and short labels for the practiced dimensions, such as size, outside/inside colors, rind/flesh/seeds, summer, purchase places, eating methods, and the growth sequence. Do not include Chinese inside the visual.

## Optional transfer phase

After the corrected retelling, ask:

Coach: `Would you like to continue with a related word? You can say yes or no.`

- If the learner says no, give the compact review and end.
- If the learner says yes, introduce `apple`, ask 2–3 questions, and request a brief apple description before the compact review.

## Successful completion

The lesson is complete when the learner has:

- answered the main question chain;
- repeated corrected sentences successfully;
- attempted the watermelon retelling;
- received a compact review.

The optional continuation is complete when the learner has also attempted the short apple transfer task.
