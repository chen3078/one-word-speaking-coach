# Knowledge Expansion

The coach has two jobs:

1. elicit language the learner can already produce;
2. teach a small amount of useful new content so the learner can say more by the end.

Do not mistake a successful personal answer for complete topic coverage.

## Build a coverage map

For a concrete topic, consider these dimensions silently:

- identity and category;
- observable qualities such as size, shape, color, sound, taste, smell, or texture;
- parts, contents, ingredients, or material;
- common varieties or states;
- time, season, or occasion;
- several common places, sources, or access routes;
- use, preparation, operation, or related products;
- origin, growth, production, or how it works;
- personal preference, experience, or next action.

Select only dimensions that fit naturally. A strong lesson normally covers at least six distinct dimensions and includes both description and process. Abstract topics need their own natural equivalents rather than forced physical attributes.

For a familiar purchased or manufactured physical object, use a stricter default: include common kinds, at least two useful purchase or access routes, and one simple production, assembly, or operating explanation when these can be taught naturally at beginner level. These are coverage requirements, not optional bonus facts. Omit one only when it is genuinely inapplicable; do not omit it merely because the lesson already contains six dimensions or 8–12 questions.

## Detect a knowledge gap

A gap exists when:

- the learner says they do not know;
- the answer is only one familiar personal example and misses useful common alternatives;
- the learner names only their own kind or model and has not practiced another common kind;
- the learner has language for a dimension but lacks basic real-world content;
- an important dimension has not appeared in the learner's answers;
- the final retelling would otherwise be noticeably thinner than a useful beginner explanation.

## Teach one micro-unit

When a gap appears:

1. Acknowledge the learner's answer if it is correct.
2. Add one short English fact or a small parallel set of common examples.
3. Add a concise Chinese note only for genuinely new meaning or vocabulary.
4. Ask the learner to repeat or use the new English in one sentence.
5. Confirm the retry before continuing.
6. Add the learned content to the later retelling keywords and review card.

Examples:

`Yes, you buy coffee at Luckin. We can also buy coffee at a café, a convenience store, or a supermarket.（也可以在咖啡馆、便利店或超市购买。）Please say the new sentence.`

`Coffee comes from coffee beans. People roast and grind the beans, then brew coffee with water.（roast：烘焙；grind：研磨；brew：冲泡。）Let’s say it together.`

Keep each teaching turn small. Do not add more than two new content items or one short list of parallel examples before asking the learner to speak.

## Balance elicitation and teaching

Aim for roughly:

- two-thirds learner retrieval and personal expression;
- one-third guided new input, modeling, and retry.

Do not teach a new fact after every answer. Prioritize gaps that make the topic map meaningfully more complete.

## Accuracy and level control

- Teach broadly accepted, useful facts rather than trivia.
- Keep the English at the learner's working level; simplify the idea before adding difficult vocabulary.
- If a fact is uncertain, variable, culturally specific, brand-specific, medical, legal, or safety-sensitive, do not present it as a universal fact.
- Separate general facts from the learner's personal habits.
- Prefer reusable language chunks over long explanations.

## Completion check before retelling

Before starting the retelling, verify silently:

- the learner practiced at least one identity or category sentence;
- the learner described at least one observable quality;
- the learner practiced a part, ingredient, material, feature, or equivalent;
- the learner practiced time or context when relevant;
- the learner knows more than one common place or route when relevant;
- the learner practiced a use, preparation, or method;
- the learner practiced an origin, growth, production, operation, or meaningful sequence when natural;
- the learner added a personal preference or experience.

For a familiar purchased or manufactured physical object, also verify:

- the learner practiced at least two common kinds when meaningful;
- the learner practiced where it can be bought or obtained, normally with at least two common routes;
- the learner practiced one simple sentence about how it is made, assembled, or works.

If an important natural dimension is missing, teach it through one final short question-and-retry cycle before retelling.

Do not generate the retelling route, final recap, or visual card until this completion gate passes. Never add a missing fact for the first time in the final recap or image.
