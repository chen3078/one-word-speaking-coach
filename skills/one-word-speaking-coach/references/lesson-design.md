# Lesson Design and Knowledge Expansion

## The teacher has two jobs

1. Elicit language and personal facts the learner can already express.
2. Teach useful related vocabulary, situations, and simple real-world knowledge so the learner can say more by the end.

A correct personal answer does not complete a topic dimension. Do not let every next question be a narrow reaction to the latest answer. Follow the silent coverage plan and connect the transitions naturally.

Aim for roughly 60% learner retrieval and 40% guided input, modeling, and retry. A normal lesson should include 3-6 meaningful expansions, selected for usefulness rather than trivia.

## Professional expansion standard

Choose teacher-led additions as a professional English teacher designing a coherent beginner lesson. An expansion should improve what the learner can actually say, not merely add an interesting fact.

Prioritize additions that:

- supply high-frequency everyday vocabulary or a reusable sentence pattern;
- fill an important gap in the topic coverage map;
- connect naturally to the learner's most recent accepted answer;
- distinguish a personal fact from simple, broadly accepted general knowledge;
- support a realistic situation, action, comparison, problem, care instruction, or choice;
- can be explained and practiced at the learner's demonstrated level.

Keep at least 80% of the language at or below the demonstrated level. Introduce at most one main new language target in a micro-unit; add a second item only when it forms one natural chunk. Prefer concrete examples, contrast, and function over dictionary-style definitions or trivia. Never present uncertain, culturally narrow, technical, or age-inappropriate information as essential knowledge.

Use this teaching sequence for each expansion:

1. bridge briefly from a practiced answer or a detected coverage gap;
2. teach one useful idea in one short, natural model sentence;
3. proactively give a concise Chinese gloss for every likely unfamiliar teacher-introduced content word;
4. require the learner to say the complete English model;
5. correct one priority issue and keep the item pending until the retry passes;
6. record only the final accepted sentence as a `P` item.

Across the lesson, vary the value of the additions when the topic permits: one useful word or chunk, one reusable pattern or contrast, and one practical piece of topic knowledge. Do not turn expansion into a lecture, a vocabulary dump, or a sequence of facts the learner never speaks.

## Coverage map

Choose about 8-11 natural dimensions and 10-14 questions:

- identity and category;
- appearance or sensory qualities;
- parts, contents, ingredients, materials, or key features;
- common kinds, states, or alternatives;
- function and practical uses;
- time, occasion, frequency, or place;
- how to use, operate, prepare, clean, or care for it;
- where to find, buy, borrow, or obtain it;
- how it grows, is made, is assembled, or works;
- common problem, safety situation, comparison, or choice;
- personal habit, experience, preference, or reason.

Select only dimensions that fit. Do not force physical attributes onto abstract topics.

For a familiar purchased or manufactured object, normally cover all of these when beginner-friendly:

- at least two common kinds;
- material or important parts;
- function and an operating sequence;
- at least two places or routes for obtaining it;
- one simple sentence about how it is made, assembled, or works;
- keeping, care, safety, or a common problem;
- a personal experience or preference.

## Structured object-question spine

For a familiar everyday object, normally design a coherent 11-14-question path from these roles:

1. what it is or its simple category;
2. overall appearance;
3. color or another observable quality;
4. material;
5. useful parts, including a meaningful absence when relevant;
6. two or more common kinds;
7. primary use;
8. frequency or occasion;
9. where it is kept or found;
10. how it is cleaned, cared for, or operated;
11. where it can be bought or obtained;
12. how it is made, assembled, or works;
13. a problem or safety point when natural;
14. personal preference and reason.

Combine overlapping roles and omit artificial ones, but do not replace the spine with a chain of narrow follow-up questions about the learner's latest sentence. If the learner cannot supply general knowledge, teach a concise model with proactive Chinese glosses, require a retry, and store the successful response under that question. A question answered through a teacher model remains a `Q` entry; use a `P` entry only for a meaningful addition taught without a question.

## Natural progression

Move from easy and observable toward useful action and personal connection:

`identity -> looks -> parts/material -> kinds -> use -> time/place -> process/care/problem -> personal experience/opinion`

For a very familiar concrete object, a simple identity question may come first when the coach is ready to model the answer. Otherwise begin with an easy personal or observable question, then practice identity or category early in the lesson.

## Gap detection

Teach a micro-unit when:

- the learner says they do not know;
- the learner gives only one familiar personal example and common alternatives matter;
- an important topic dimension has not appeared;
- the learner knows a fact but lacks the everyday English for it;
- the final topic map would otherwise omit a practical beginner concept.

Separate general knowledge from the learner's personal facts. Teach broadly accepted daily knowledge, not uncertain trivia. Keep at least 80% of the language at or below the demonstrated level.

## Completion gate

Before retelling, verify silently that the learner has successfully practiced:

- identity or category;
- an observable quality;
- a part, material, feature, ingredient, or natural equivalent;
- a use, method, or action sequence;
- a time, place, or context when relevant;
- more than one common kind or access route when useful;
- production, operation, care, safety, or a meaningful process when natural;
- a personal habit, experience, preference, or reason;
- at least three meaningful teacher-led additions in a normal lesson;
- every likely unfamiliar teacher-introduced word has been proactively glossed in Chinese and practiced in English.

If a required natural dimension is missing, return to one short question-and-retry or teaching-and-retry cycle. Never compensate by inserting unpracticed facts only into the retelling, written review, or image.
