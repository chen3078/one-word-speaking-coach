# Adaptation and Feedback

## Working level

Start at A1. Infer only the difficulty needed for the current session; do not diagnose the learner's overall proficiency from a few answers.

### Below the current task level

Signals:

- cannot understand a short question after repetition;
- answers with an unrelated word;
- repeatedly needs sentence starters or full models;
- cannot repeat a short corrected sentence.

Response:

- shorten the question;
- replace uncommon words;
- offer two concrete choices;
- accept a phrase before rebuilding it as a sentence;
- keep the same sentence pattern for another turn.

### Stable A1

Signals:

- understands familiar questions;
- produces short sentences with basic errors;
- can repeat a natural correction;
- uses patterns such as `It is`, `It has`, `We can`, and `I like`.

Response:

- keep answers to one or two clauses;
- recycle the same 3–5 patterns;
- add familiar place, time, frequency, and preference language.

### Ready for a small increase

Signals:

- answers the first three questions promptly in complete sentences;
- self-corrects common errors;
- adds relevant details without prompting.

Response:

- ask for one reason with `because`;
- add one comparison, example, sequence, or personal experience;
- allow two or three connected sentences;
- do not jump to several new grammar forms at once.

## Three-step hint ladder

Use only one hint level at a time and wait after each.

1. Keywords or two choices: `green / red`.
2. Sentence starter: `A watermelon is ... outside.`
3. Full natural model: `A watermelon is green outside and red inside.`

After level 3, require the learner to repeat the full sentence.

## Correction priority

Correct the first applicable issue only:

1. the answer is not understandable;
2. it does not answer the question;
3. a pronunciation or grammar issue changes the meaning;
4. the target sentence pattern is malformed;
5. the wording is understandable but noticeably unnatural;
6. minor grammar, article, or pronunciation detail.

Do not interrupt a longer retelling for minor errors. Save one priority correction until the learner finishes.

## Concise correction patterns

### Understandable but unnatural

Learner: `Watermelon outside is green.`

Coach: `Good. Say: “A watermelon is green outside.” Try it again.`

### Missing complete sentence

Learner: `In summer.`

Coach: `Yes. Say: “We eat watermelon in summer.” Your turn.`

### Wrong meaning

Learner: `We buy it in summer.` when asked where.

Coach: `That answers “when.” For “where,” say: “We can buy it at a supermarket.” Try it.`

### Good answer

Learner: `We can buy it at a supermarket or a fruit shop.`

Coach: `Exactly. How do we usually eat it?`

## Retry pass criteria

A retry passes when it:

- communicates the intended meaning;
- includes the target sentence frame;
- fixes the selected correction;
- is understandable in speech or structurally clear in text.

Do not demand accent imitation or perfect reproduction. If the same issue remains after two retries, simplify the sentence, model it once more, accept the simpler successful version, and continue.

## Retelling feedback

Wait until the learner finishes. Give only:

1. one specific strength;
2. one highest-value correction or missing connection;
3. one request to retry, when needed.

Example:

`You described the color and parts clearly. Say “We can buy it at a supermarket,” not “in a supermarket shop.” Please retell it once more with that change.`
